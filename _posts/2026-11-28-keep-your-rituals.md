I've had many habits and lifestyle changes rise and fall. Like casual bouldering with my school friends once every 2 weeks or so. Or blogging- when I first started blogging, it was a weekly endeavour. I even started playing board games at one point, on Fridays with friends from uni.

None of these rituals lasted! Usually because they were not fun enough to warrant investing the effort on a regular basis. I am not a huge fan of board games. And even though I like bouldering, I have to go all the way into the Inner West to climb. Blogging takes hours and because it is creative it can't be forced or regular. Even badminton training takes half my weekend to prepare for and recover from. So there are months at a time where I fall out of it.

Despite all of this, one ritual has persisted since I started it 3 years ago. My morning runs to work with Josh. I catch the train halfway to work, where he lives, and we leave from the station. Sometimes he gets on the train with me, we drop our stuff off at the office, and do a loop.

Running still happens because the ROI is so high that it justifies itself. It is so integrated into my life that if I have to make a big change like moving houses or jobs, I'll mourn losing the convenience of the weekly run.

Why is the ROI so high?

The effort is so, so low:

- I already go into the office at least 3 times a week. I barely have to vary my routine to make the time for running. Going to sleep and waking up an hour earlier is usually enough.
- I already run. Running with Josh is nothing more than scheduling it at a fixed time.
- The office has excellent facilities, there are spacious, clean showers, and basic breakfast (eggs, toast, coffee, cereal) available post-run.
- Running is free! It costs nothing no matter how long you run for.
- Running is easy. If you are unfamiliar with long distance running, it is closer to walking than it is to sprinting. The whole point is to stay in 'zone 2' which is aerobic and low-intensity activity. The goal when running is for your leg muscles to give before your heart does. Otherwise, you are doing it wrong. And for the distances we run I barely feel it.

And the return is pretty damn high:

- I hang out with Josh, who I don't spend a lot of time with outside of work. We catch up, vent, tell funny stories. Friend stuff. All without feeling like sacrificing time, which is important when time is at a premium. 
- Running is healthy. 1 hour of running, 8 - 12 km, is my daily exercise.
- It is the only hour in the day when I'm away from my phone.

Try to make your rituals as convenient as possible so that they last. The easiest way to build friendships is to do things together that you would be doing individually anyway.
