Since I gained independence after starting uni I've had many habits and lifestyle changes rise and fall. For example, I started casually bouldering with my school friends once every 2 weeks or so. When I first started blogging here it was a weekly weekend thing. And I even started playing board games on Fridays with friends from uni.

But none of these rituals lasted! Usually because they were not fun enough to warrant investing the effort on a regular basis. I am not a huge fan of board games. And even though I like bouldering, I have to go all the way into the Inner West which is a day trip. Blogging takes hours and because it is creative it can't be forced or regular. Even badminton, that I have been playing for almost 9 years now, takes half my weekend to prepare for and recover from. So there are months at a time where I fall out of it.

Despite all of this, one ritual has persisted since I started it 3 years ago. My morning runs to work with Josh. I catch the train halfway to work, where he lives, and I meet him at the station and we go from there. Sometimes he gets on the train with me, we drop our stuff off at the office, and do a loop.

Running has persisted above all else because the ROI is so high that it justifies itself. It is so integrated into my life that if I have to make a big change like moving houses or jobs, I really will mourn losing the convenience of the weekly run.

Why is the ROI so high?

The effort is so, so low:

- I already go into the office at least 3 times a week. I barely have to vary my routine to make the time for running- going to sleep and waking up an hour earlier is usually enough.
- I already run. All this ritual does is schedule it at a fixed time.
- The office has excellent facilities, there are spacious, clean showers, and basic breakfast (eggs, toast, coffee, cereal) post-run.
- Running is free! It costs essentially nothing no matter how long you run for.
- Running is easy. If you are unfamiliar with long distance running, it is closer to walking than it is to sprinting. The whole point is to stay in zone 2, which is aerobic and low-intensity. The goal when running is for your leg muscles to give before your heart does. Otherwise, you are doing it wrong.

And the return is pretty damn high:

- I get to see Josh, who I don't spend a lot of time with outside of work. We catch up, talk about our lives, vent, tell funny stories. Friend stuff. All without feeling like sacrificing time, which is important when time is at a premium. 
- Running is healthy. 1 hour of running, 8-12 km, is my daily exercise.
- It is the only hour in the day when I absolutely cannot be looking at my phone. And don't feel the need to.

The TL;DR is that one of the easiest ways to build friendships is to do things together that you would be doing individually anyways. The higher the ROI the more sustainable it is.
