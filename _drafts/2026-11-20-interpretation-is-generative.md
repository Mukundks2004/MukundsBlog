We all know that interpreting is generative in different ways. An audience brings a perspective, a bias, a filter, a lense to what they consume- they 'generate' a novel *interpretation*. Philosophically, I think that the act of thinking about something 'creates' an idea in this abstract space of ideas that is the human condition (? What is this collection of ideas ). An audience also adds meaning that creators welcome and develop their community.

I want to offer a spin on this: the people who generate, who film movies, write books, do improv, they're the ones who interpret. There is a bi-implication, both premises imply both consequents (antecedent?). This is because people who generate necessarily develop the same skills it takes to interpret, their brain is altered to force them to recognise artistic choices and parallels from their own experiences. The same way that learning to drive forever changes your experience as a passenger. 

I'll go further and say that generaters (generators?) are nothing more than 'real' people. People who live a certain reality- the artists who are 'real'- they understand the rules of that reality better, so they are able to bend them and dance around them which makes for such rich interpretations. It is a very specific and curious expression of expertise. (what is she an expert on? life? her lives?)

Take *Lana Del (del?) Rey*, my favourite artist. She is as *real* as it gets. In her first (confirm) album *Born to Die: Paradise Edition (?)* she plays all these characters, such as the Crooning Girlfriend, the groomed Lolita, the Hipster from Brooklyn (link tihs to article and make sure wording of each character matches).

Every one of them is a variant of herself, a lived reality, a personality from a past whose inspiration (word for place where the personality came from) still lives within Lana. She's as real as a heavy metal rockstar with the groupies, sex, drugs, and partying. She's not posing or commenting on a stereotype from the other side of the glass. She's just playing up parts of herself. This is the reason that she is an artist I *follow*, and not just someone whose music I enjoy.
